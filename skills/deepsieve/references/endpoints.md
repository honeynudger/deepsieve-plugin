# DeepSieve /v1 endpoints

All requests: `Authorization: Bearer ds_live_...`. Base URL = the user's
deployment origin. Every response carries `X-Request-Id` and `RateLimit-*`.
Every route is listed once, in full, exactly as served — grep this file for
the path you are about to call. Path parameters use the contract's names.

## Which Blueprint (read this once)

An org can hold several Blueprints, and a call on the wrong one is not an error — it is a plausible
answer to a question you did not ask. So runs, estimates and webhooks REQUIRE `blueprint_id` (the `id`
`GET /v1/blueprints` lists): missing or blank is `400 invalid_request` with `param: "blueprint_id"`, refused
before anything is charged or an `Idempotency-Key` is used. Only a `dry_run` may omit it. Reads and reports
take it optionally; omitted = your **ambient** Blueprint, the active one: `is_active` in
`GET /v1/blueprints`, and `blueprint` in `GET /v1/me`. **Run, estimate, report, webhook,
chat and monitoring responses carry `blueprint: {id, name}`**: check it is the one you meant before you
act on the answer. (The `/v1/blueprints/{blueprint_id}/…` reads are addressed by id already; there,
`blueprint` is the Blueprint's definition, not this reference. `GET /v1/export` and a `dry_run`'s poll
carry none.) An id you
already hold (a run, report, webhook endpoint or chat action id) is found in whichever Blueprint you can
read, and the response names it; an id in a Blueprint you cannot read is a `404`, never a `403`. On a very
large account the search stops after `v1.id_lookup_max_blueprints` Blueprints (20 by default); that `404`
says the search was cut short, so it is NOT a confirmed miss: list the Blueprint you expect it in, with
`?blueprint_id=`, to find it there.

## Renamed parameters (read this if a response warns you)

`workspace_id` is renamed **`blueprint_id`** (same value) on `GET /v1/export` and `POST /v1/keys`, and
`workspace_ids` is renamed **`blueprint_ids`** on `GET /v1/members` (gh #1282). For 30 days the old INPUT name
still works, and every response to a call that SENT it says so, including a call that then fails: `Deprecation` and `Sunset` headers (the `Sunset`
date is when it stops), a `Link` to the changelog, an `X-DeepSieve-Warning` header, and a
`warnings: [{code: "parameter_renamed", message}]` entry on the key or in the export's metadata record. After
the sunset, the old name gets `400 parameter_renamed`.
- **On the warning:** switch to `blueprint_id` now.
- **On `400 parameter_renamed`:** resend the same request with `blueprint_id`.
- **Either way, ask your human before you change saved skills or instructions** that use `workspace_id`.
  They are the human's, so ask their permission to update them to `blueprint_id`.
- **Response fields:** `ApiKey.workspace_id` and the members' `workspace_ids` still arrive, deprecated, with
  no warning (reading them is invisible to the server). Read `blueprint_id` / `blueprint_ids` instead. The
  old fields are removed in a later, separately announced step.

## Identity & schema
| Endpoint | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `GET /v1/me` | who this key is — the auth + entitlement check. `{object:"identity", app_url, org_id, user_id, role, workspace_schema, blueprint, auth_kind, scopes, plan, balance_cents, plan_run_credits, free_run_available, subscription, dpa, capabilities}`. `blueprint` (`{id, name}`, or `null` when the org has no usable Blueprint) is the Blueprint your un-addressed calls act on — its `id` is what every `blueprint_id` takes; `workspace_schema` is an internal name no other call accepts. `role` ∈ viewer\|member\|admin\|owner; `auth_kind` says how the caller authenticated (`api_key`, `session`, `oauth`, `open`); `scopes: null` = a pre-scoping key with full role authority; `org_id: null` = open single-tenant mode. `app_url` is where this deployment's web app lives (no trailing slash) — use it to hand your human a link they can open, see "Handing a schema change to your human" below |
| ↳ the TWO balances | `balance_cents` is prepaid dollars; `plan_run_credits` (`{available, next_expiry}`) is the plan's included runs, spent FIRST in whole interactions, with the prepaid balance paying the rest of a run (`funding.rail: mixed` on the estimate). Either being `null` means UNKNOWN, never zero — a credit-funded subscriber can show `balance_cents: 0` and still afford runs |
| ↳ `model_allowance` | the ORGANIZATION's model-token allowance for chat, onboarding and query parsing (a research run never counts): `{reset: monthly\|lifetime, resets_on, input_tokens: {used, limit}, output_tokens: {used, limit}, exhausted, near}`. At a monthly cap those calls are `429 model_allowance_exhausted` (wait for `Retry-After`). A free org's allowance is lifetime: `402 plan_upgrade_required` with `param: "model_allowance"` (ask your human to subscribe; don't retry). `null` = unknown, never zero |
| ↳ `free_run_available` | is this org's ONE free research run still unspent? Check it before you decide you cannot afford a first run — an unsubscribed org with `balance_cents: 0` may still be able to run, for free. `true` only for an org with no plan, not an expired beta, not a closed account in retention, and that has not claimed the run yet — the same conditions the charge gate applies. It covers a STANDARD run only: it never funds `depth: max`. `null` means eligibility could not be determined (billing inactive, or the read failed) and never "no free run" — do not treat it as `false`. Whether a SPECIFIC request would consume it is `uses_free_run` on the estimate |
| ↳ `subscription` | when money moves, before it moves: `{status, cancel_at_period_end, access_through, trial}`. `status` ∈ `none` (no plan: not always a prospect, see the off-plan fields below) \| `past_due` (a charge was declined: ask your human to update the card in the app, unless `cancel_at_period_end`: then it is not retried) \| `ended` (a trial cancelled before its first charge, now over: nothing charged, access ended) \| `trialing` (in the free trial, or just past it with the first charge not paid yet) \| `active`. `trial` is the free trial: `eligible: true` means the org may START one (it depends on who asks: the owner of another org that had a trial is not offered one), and `offer_days` is its length. Checkout takes a card, so tell your human and hand them `{app_url}/pricing` (never start a checkout yourself). During a trial, `ends_on`, `days_left` and `first_charge_on` / `first_charge_cents` say when and how much the card will be charged (read `days_left` rather than counting). The first charge's fields are `null` once the trial is cancelled (a cancelled trial is never charged), unless that charge is already in flight. After `ends_on`: `conversion_pending` (not gone out yet; a cancel still stops it), `conversion_submitted` (in flight; it can't be stopped) `conversion_failed` (declined) or `ended_without_charge` (cancelled and over, nothing charged). `subscription: null` (or `trial: null`) = unknown (or open mode), never "none". With `status: none`, the off-plan fields tell a closed or expired org from a prospect (gh #3278), checked in this order: `expired_from_plan` set (with `expired_from_plan_label`, e.g. "Builders Trial") means its term ended, so research stays readable and exportable with Report Chat, but there are no new runs and no free run; `retention_until` set means the account is CLOSED, read-only and deleted on that date (the date stays set after the deletion, so once it has passed treat the research as gone), with no free run and top-ups refused (`account_closed`). Both null means a prospect. Tell your human which, and the date, and hand them `{app_url}/pricing`. A plan is never off-plan |
| ↳ `dpa` | the org's Data Processing Agreement: `{current_version, offer, accepted, update_available}`. `current_version: null` means no DPA is published. `offer` is `click_through` (Pro and Enterprise: an owner or admin accepts it in the app) or `on_request` (ask and it is sent). `accepted` is `{version, content_sha256, customer_role, accepted_by_email, accepted_at, channel}` or `null` (`channel`: `click_through` in the app, or `on_request`, a signed copy we recorded); `update_available: true` means a newer version is offered while the accepted one stays in force. **Read-only: there is no API to accept it.** Accepting is a contract act your human does in Settings, so hand them `{app_url}/settings` and `{app_url}/dpa` to read. `dpa: null` = no organization, or the state could not be read |
| ↳ `capabilities` | what the plan allows, so you can branch BEFORE a 402. Booleans: `max_research` (may run `depth: max`, Pro+), `webhooks` (may create/manage webhook endpoints, Pro+), `audit_log` (may read the audit trail, Pro+), `teammates` (self-serve teammate invitations, Pro+ — `false` means inviting refuses `plan_upgrade_required`, an upgrade moment, not an error; `GET /v1/members` works on every plan), `priority_queue` (runs scheduled ahead of lower tiers, Pro+), `sso` (SAML/OIDC SSO + SCIM, Enterprise), `white_label` (Enterprise). Plus `blueprint_limit` (int, or `null` = unlimited / open mode). Unsubscribed (`plan: null`) every boolean is `false`; open mode every boolean is `true`. Check the key you need; the set is derived from the plan schema and grows with it |
| `GET /v1/me/email-preferences` | whether your human receives product and follow-up emails (the getting-started nudge, the winback, the monthly summary; receipts, security and billing mail ignore it). `{object:"email_preferences", product_emails, refused_at, refused_via, refuses_all_email, offered_at}`. `refused_via` ∈ signup\|settings\|unsubscribe\|notice. `product_emails: true` means no refusal is on file, not that an email will arrive (one also needs `offered_at`: your human was shown the choice in the app). Always the key owner's own preference; no scope. `403 access_denied` for a key with no person behind it |
| `PUT /v1/me/email-preferences` | **only when your human asked.** `{product_emails: false}` refuses (recorded as a settings choice). `true` is refused from a key or MCP session with `403 product_emails_need_a_person`: turning email back on withdraws your human's own refusal, so they do it at `{app_url}/settings`. Idempotent, audited. Same answer as the `GET` |
| `POST /v1/subscription/cancel` | **only when your human asked.** Cancels at the end of the period; nothing new is charged after it (a trial is never charged; a declined charge is not retried), except a first charge already in flight (`trial.conversion_submitted`), which goes ahead. → `{object:"subscription", ...the /v1/me subscription block, retry:null}`. A repeat changes nothing. Needs `billing:cancel` + admin. `409 no_subscription` (no plan); `409 billing_update_in_progress` (retriable, `Retry-After`) |
| `POST /v1/subscription/resume` | **only when your human asked.** Undoes a cancellation while the period runs; a trial keeps its original first-charge date; a past-due account gets ONE immediate charge attempt (`retry`). `retry: "awaiting_confirmation"`: a payment is still open, usually your human's bank asking them to confirm it (3-D Secure); nothing was charged, and `retry_on` is the first day a retry can go through (`null` when none is scheduled, and on every other outcome). Needs `billing:write` + admin. **No MCP session can resume** (it re-arms a charge): send your human (an admin) to `{app_url}/billing` (`app_url` from `GET /v1/me`), or they can use an API key with `billing:write`. `409 subscription_ended`: it's over, hand your human `{app_url}/pricing` |
| `GET /v1/blueprints/{blueprint_id}` | **read your Blueprint's schema** — its entities and their columns (**call before any data read**, and re-read rather than hardcoding: the customer can change their Blueprint). `blueprint` carries `{domain_name, entities}`, where `domain_name` is what this Blueprint researches. This is also the source of truth for "is X a **Blueprint** column" — not `/v1/reports/columns`, which answers a different question. (Row reads additionally carry `id` and `updated_at`, which are not Blueprint columns and are not listed here.) |
| `GET /v1/blueprints` | **which Blueprints this org has**, and which one the unaddressed endpoints answer for. `{data:[{id, name, status, is_active, has_data, onboarding_session_id}], has_more, next_cursor}`. Not paginated in practice — one row per Blueprint. Call this once if you might be working somewhere with more than one; `id` is what every Blueprint-scoped read below takes |

## Research runs
| Endpoint | Notes |
|---|---|
| `POST /v1/research/runs/estimate` | `{blueprint_id, depth, seeds?, query?, additive_to?, reuse_existing?, temporal_cutoff?, allowed_sources?, excluded_sources?, search_hints?, check_urls?}` (`blueprint_id` REQUIRED: missing is `400 invalid_request`) → `blueprint` (the Blueprint priced — send the same `blueprint_id` you will run with), `run_price_cents`, `entities`, `interactions`, `balance_cents`, `plan_run_credits`, `affordable`, `uses_free_run`, `allowed` + `reason`, `warnings[]` (`{code, message}`; see `allowed_sources` below). Ask BEFORE you buy. **The quote is the create's own price for the same request** (one function decides both): seeds folded as one entity, already in an `additive_to` parent, priced out by `reuse_existing` (returned as `reuse_predicted`, the create's own field) or trimmed by a known free run are not priced. Send the same `reuse_existing` and `temporal_cutoff` you will create with. A request the create would refuse before charging (every seed already in the parent, or every seed predicted held under `reuse_existing`) is refused here with the create's own `422` and message. 🛑 **Do not halt on `affordable: false` without reading `uses_free_run`**: when it is `true` the run costs nothing, `affordable` is `true` and `shortfall_cents` is `0` whatever the balance says — `run_price_cents` still quotes the DOLLAR price, so never infer funding from the price. `uses_free_run: null` means eligibility was unverifiable, and then `affordable` and `shortfall_cents` are `null` too — unknown, not unaffordable. 🛑 **`affordable: false` with no plan** (`plan: null` and `uses_free_run: false`: the free run is used, the Private Beta has ended, the account is closed, or it is a Max run) is refused WHATEVER the balance: only a plan opens it, never a top-up, and `shortfall_cents` is then just the dollar gap, `0` when the balance covers the price. Tell your human they need a plan; do not buy credit for it. A free run is capped at one interaction's worth of entities and the rest are DROPPED, disclosed as `free_run` (`entity_cap`, `requested`, `researched`, `dropped`) when a trim would apply; `entities` and `run_price_cents` then count only what is researched. When free-run eligibility is unknown no trim is applied, so the quote is the higher number. With `additive_to` it prices the GAP and returns `skipped_already_held`. A `query` estimate echoes an `intake` block (incl. any `focus`/`avoid` column narrowing) — narrowing changes tokens, not price. **URL seeds: send `check_urls: true`** whenever the person gave you URLs. `url_outcomes` then says per URL `found` (count), `none_found` or `unreadable`. A URL is read to FIND what to research, so a page naming nothing adds nothing and is still priced. Not instant (several fetches and model calls per URL, against the org's model allowance; at most 25 URLs), never changes the price; without it you get `check_urls_hint` and nothing is read. `warnings[]` adds `allowed_sources_seed_hosts_only` when the allowed sources are only those pages' own sites, except when `seed_mode` is `as_typed` (each URL is then its own target, so nothing is searched by name) Seeds that are one entity ("Acme" and "acme", "Fund X, L.P." and "Fund X", a name you merged into another, two spellings of one held record) are priced once, and named in `folded_duplicates: [{seed, kept_as, reason}]` (`reason`: `case`, `same_record`, `legal_form`, `alias`; an open string), present only when something folded. Show it to your human before buying: it is why the count is lower than the seeds you sent. |
| `POST /v1/research/runs` | at most one of `{query}` (natural-language intent — PARSED into specific subjects, a whole-domain sweep, or `400 invalid_request` if it can't be read as a request; parsing spends nothing) or `{seeds:[...]}` (URLs or names, taken verbatim) — both is `400 invalid_request`; **neither** starts a broad sweep derived from the named Blueprint's schema (one interaction, like any broad run) and the run's `derived_brief` says what it swept. A query's 202 carries `intake` (how it was read: `decision`, `summary`; a column narrowing as `intake.column_scope` = `focus`/`avoid` + `intake.column_fields` + `intake.column_fields_span` (each field → every entity it affects, the effective global scope); a real column you named that is never researched — `name` / `primary_source_urls` — under `intake.column_fields_ignored`, DISTINCT from `intake.ignored_refs` which lists any `@entity.column` the Blueprint lacks). Optional `guidance`, `depth: standard\|max`, `monitored`, `additive_to`, `reuse_existing`, `temporal_cutoff`, `allowed_sources`, `seed_labels` (`{seed: label}`, every key one of `seeds`; with a `query` the parser reads `P01: <url>` labels itself), `dry_run`. **`expected_total_cents?: int`** (strict) BINDS the create to the estimate: send its `run_price_cents` once your human approves; a run that would now cost MORE is `409 run_price_changed` (`param: expected_total_cents`, `details`: `expected_total_cents`, `total_price_cents`, `interactions`, `unit_price_cents`), nothing created or charged; ask your human, then resend with `total_price_cents`. The same or a lower price starts as usual (reuse, the free-run trim and `additive_to` only lower it); how it is funded never triggers `run_price_changed`. Separately, a run handled as your free run that would be paid from plan credits or the balance is `409 run_funding_changed` (retriable), with or without the bind, nothing charged (the run is recorded as failed, not charged): estimate again and confirm the new price with your human. A `query` the parser reads as URLs plus "use only these" (allowed sources only those pages' hosts) keeps each URL as **its own target**, in your order: the run says `seed_mode: "as_typed"`. A `seeds` request is always `"discover"` (each URL is read to find what to research). **`blueprint_id` is REQUIRED unless `dry_run`** (the Blueprint to research; missing or blank is `400 invalid_request`, `param: "blueprint_id"`, before anything is charged; a dry run without it uses your ambient one). Send `Idempotency-Key`. → 202 + `Location`; the resource's `blueprint` names the Blueprint it is running on. A 202 has already started and charged the run, so confirm the Blueprint BEFORE it, on the estimate or a `dry_run`; if the 202 names the wrong one, cancel at once The 202 (and a `dry_run`) names the same `folded_duplicates` the estimate named, present only when something folded; `kept_as` is the spelling the run stores in `seeds`. |
| ↳ `additive_to` | Extend an existing report: entities it already holds with in-TTL evidence are skipped, so you pay for the GAP. Use this for a recurring top-up instead of re-sending the whole list. It JOINS the report's monitoring: `monitored` is IGNORED with it (silently), and its new dossiers are free for 30 days from this run, then bill with the report |
| ↳ `reuse_existing` | Keep data the Blueprint ALREADY holds (in-TTL evidence) instead of re-researching it — carried in with its sources at no run credit; the rest is researched normally. The canonical case is re-submitting a watchlist on a schedule. The 202's `reuse_predicted` (and `reused_existing`, same value) lists what the price left out; the run's `reuse_carried` is what was actually carried, and `being_refreshed` what a running refresh already covers. A predicted entity reuse cannot carry is researched at no extra charge, up to a per-run limit; the rest are listed in `reuse_not_researched` |
| ↳ `temporal_cutoff` | `{date, kind, column?}` — a dated ask the run ENFORCES, rather than prose it is merely asked to honour. `date` is a LOWER bound (`YYYY-MM-DD`, never in the future). `kind: entity` = which SUBJECTS qualify ("launched in the last 12 months"): checked at persist against `column`, a `date`-typed column of the Blueprint, and subjects without that date are kept. `kind: evidence` = how FRESH a value must be ("the latest headcount"): a search time-window plus a per-cell check against each fact's source date; undated values are kept. With a `query` the parser derives one; a value you send WINS over it, so answer an `intake.clarifications` about a vague "recent" by sending the date outright. A `date` after today, or an entity `column` that is not a date column, is a `422` naming the columns you could have used — never a silent no-op. What the run enforces comes back as `intake.temporal_cutoff` and on the run resource, in the SAME shape, so you can echo it straight back on the next create |
| ↳ `allowed_sources` | `["dot.state.al.us", "*.gov", "https://host/path/*", "blueprint:primary"]` — the sources the run ENFORCES: research is steered toward them, but the guarantee is that a cell cited only from sites outside the list is **not written** and its URLs come back as a lead. A host allows its subdomains; `*.suffix` allows any host under it (a bare public suffix such as `*.com` is a `400`); a URL prefix is host-exact; `blueprint:primary` / `blueprint:trade` expand to the Blueprint's declared hosts at create time. The hosts of URL seeds are always allowed. `[]` is a `400 invalid_request` (`param: allowed_sources`) — it would refuse every source for full price; omit the field for no restriction. At most 50 patterns (after `blueprint:` expansion; seed hosts ride on top). `intake.allowed_sources_notice` is present when "official sources only" could not be honoured (no declared primary sources) and the clarification slot was taken: nothing is restricted, so send the list outright. With a `query` the parser may derive one from "official sources only" / "only .gov"; a value you send WINS over it. What the run enforces comes back as `intake.allowed_sources` and as `allowed_sources` + `allowed_sources_origin` (`run`\|`intake`\|`blueprint`) on the run resource. The estimate's `warnings[]` carries `allowed_sources_may_reduce_yield` whenever a list is bound (restricting sources reduces what a run returns, never what it costs) and `allowed_sources_narrow` for a very short list on a broad or many-seed run. After the run, `stage_metrics.persisting.allowed_sources` says what was excluded, by host, with the leads |
| ↳ `excluded_sources` | `["wikipedia.org", "*.example", "https://host/path/*"]` — sources the run must NEVER cite, **enforced**: a citation on one is not written, whether or not `allowed_sources` is set, and an exclusion beats an allowed source. A `query` that rules out a named site ("not Wikipedia") sets it for you (`intake.excluded_sources`); an explicit value wins: `[]` excludes nothing, even when the `query` names a site; null or omitted means the ones the parser read (none without a `query`). A bare public suffix (`*.com`), a `blueprint:` name, or a list excluding every source the run allows is `400 invalid_request` with `param: excluded_sources`, on the estimate and the dry run too. Echoed on the run resource; the run's `stage_metrics.persisting.allowed_sources` counts `citations_excluded_by_rule`. |
| ↳ `search_hints` | `{exact_phrases?: ["Request for Information"], file_types?: ["pdf"]}` — exact wording to look for and kinds of document (lowercase extensions Google documents for `filetype:`). **Soft**: the researcher runs them as extra searches, never as a filter, so nothing is dropped for missing them. A `query` that says "PDFs only" or quotes a phrase sets it for you (see `intake.search_hints`); an explicit value wins. **Check `intake.search_hints` on the estimate before you buy**: if the parser read a phrase or file type your human did not mean, send the ones to keep (`{}` keeps none) on the estimate and then the create, the same way a person removes one in the app's Review Plan dialog. They never change the price. An unusable entry is `400 invalid_request` with `param: search_hints`. Echoed on the run resource. |
| `GET /v1/research/runs/{run_id}` | found in any Blueprint you can read; `blueprint` names it. Poll until `done`; `status` ∈ queued\|running\|cancelling\|completed\|failed\|cancelled; `error` object on failure; `derived_brief` on an empty-body run; `monitored` is whether the run is monitored NOW (`true`/`false`, a toggle since creation shows; an additive run reports its report's), or `null` when monitoring does not apply to it (a cascade rerun, an update child, a run from before monitoring). `null` never means off: off is `false`. `metrics.usage_complete` qualifies the token counts: `false` = some call's provider reported no usage, so `llm_input_tokens`/`llm_output_tokens` are a FLOOR (`metrics.usage_unreported_stages` says where); `null` = unknown (older run, still running, or run across an API restart). Never read `null` as complete. `metrics.search_queries` is the searches the research agent ran, as Google reports them; `null` = not reported (older run, still running, stopped, or no count from Google), never zero searches. A re-check run (a chat `reverify` action's `reverify_run_id`) carries `check_results` once `done`: per checked value `{evidence_id, outcome, message, followup_run_id}`. Show `message` to your human; `outcome` is an open key (read an unknown one as neutral). A set `followup_run_id` (a monitoring or app Re-verify re-check that started a search; a chat re-check does not start one) is a search for the current value: GET it, and its `update_result` `{outcome, message}` says what it found. Both are `null` on other runs, until done, and on a run that ended before recording one (`status`/`error` say why) |
| `GET /v1/research/runs` | `?limit=&cursor=&status=&blueprint_id=` → `{data, has_more, next_cursor, blueprint}` — one Blueprint's runs (named, else ambient) |
| `POST /v1/research/runs/{run_id}/cancel` | stop an in-flight run. The 202's `cancel` says what happened: `refunded` (money came back: in full for a run stopped before it started; within 30s of starting, for what it had not delivered; or on a run that crashed and is being retried automatically (shown as `running`), whenever you stop it, between retry attempts too. Stopped between attempts with everything already delivered, it settles `completed`, with nothing refunded. An automatic re-research run counts as not started until it really begins, and one paid from the included re-research allowance gets the allowance back instead, with `refunded: false`), `refund_scope` = `full`\|`partial` (partial: only the part that had returned nothing; delivered results are kept and charged), `delivery` + `interactions_cancelled`/`interactions_targeted` (retry on `unattempted`). Both describe THIS call: a repeated cancel on a run already stopping reports `refunded: false` even when the first refunded, so keep the first response's values |
| `GET /v1/research/monitoring` | `?blueprint_id=` → `blueprint` + what recurring monitoring costs and where it overlaps: `monitored_units`, `billing_units` (billing today, spending daily), `duplicated_units` (paid for, but another report already keeps those entities fresh — the number to act on), per-report rows (`units`, and `units_in_free_window` + `billing_units` + `additive_windows[]` with each additive run's `free_until`: an additive run keeps its own free 30 days, so what a report bills TODAY is `billing_units`, not `units`; with `locked_until`, the exclusive 00:00Z end of the 30 days committed by turning monitoring back on after the free window, and `stop_scheduled_at`, set when it was turned off during them and still billing until then), `warnings`; and `reruns`, the included re-research used vs included this billing period, per report (`status`: `ok`, `no_allowance`, or `unavailable`, whose numbers are null, never 0) and in total (`complete: false` plus a `reruns_unavailable` warning when a report could not be read). Citation object: `{value, confidence, verdict, verdict_note, cited,
evidence_id, source_urls, retrieved_at, beside, knowledge_decided, knowledge_cited}`. The citation is always the
source of the value the cell holds. `beside` lists the rows recorded next to it
(`kind` ∈ also_stated · not_applied · withheld · non_conforming · replaced, each with its own
`verdict`, `source_hosts`, `note`, and `value` except for `withheld`; `reason`
for `not_applied`). Read a `beside` row's verdict as that row's, never the cell's. A cell
with no value row is cited from its newest `non_conforming` observation; one holding only
other `beside` rows is uncited (`evidence_id: null`).
Domain knowledge on a cell, on the citation and on each `beside` row (its own, never the
cell's): `knowledge_decided` is `[{id, outcome, range?, revision?, run_id?}]`, what an entry DECIDED
there, by code (`outcome` ∈ held_value · range_flag · source_refused · source_downscored);
`knowledge_cited` is the entry ids QA's verdict cited, a model's claim. Never add the two.
A `range_flag` recorded since 2026-10-19 carries the `range` the value breached as it stood
then; an older one has none, so never explain it with the entry's range now. A `revision`
other than the entry's current one means the entry was edited since. `run_id` is the run that
decided: a re-check can decide on an earlier run's row, so never count a decision whose `run_id`
is another run's as the run you are reporting on. A re-check REPLACES a row's decisions, so a
run's `cells_decided` (counted when it ran) can include a cell whose decisions now name another
run, or none: report the run's own counts from its knowledge read, not from the cells. Read these fields,
never the `verdict_note` wording.
`verdict` ∈ supported · leans-supported · uncertain · corrected ·
contradicted · unverifiable · not checked.

free-preview accounts. `truncated` is true only when the cap withheld a row; a table

## Domain knowledge (teach DeepSieve what your team knows)
| Endpoint | Notes |
|---|---|
| `GET /v1/blueprints/{blueprint_id}/knowledge` | the Blueprint's knowledge entries: what its owners know about the field (where reliable data lives, which sources mislead, what values mean, what to ignore, how an expert would look). `?status=&kind=&entity=&column=`. Needs `data:read` |
| `GET /v1/blueprints/{blueprint_id}/knowledge/usage` | how each entry was used across the `?runs=` most recent started runs (default 20, at most 50; every run type). Per entry one `state`: `decided` (code decided on a cell because of it), `in_scope_no_decision` (prompts carried it and no decision came of it; with `can_decide: false` the kind never decides, so it "guided" those runs), `out_of_scope` (nothing in its scope came up), or `not_checked` with `not_checked_reason` (`over_budget`, `run_in_progress`, `not_reached`, `no_run_since_written`, `not_in_force`, `no_recorded_run`). Figures: `runs_in_force`, `runs_carried`, `runs_dropped_only`, `runs_decided`, `runs_since_revision` (0 after an edit: the new wording has not run yet), `decisions` + `decided_by` (decision EVENTS, never cells), `cited` (QA's claim, never a decision), `last_decided_at`. `window` says how many runs were read and how many kept no record (`not_recorded`, counted in no entry). **Report the state, never a bare zero.** Needs `data:read` |
| `POST /v1/blueprints/{blueprint_id}/knowledge` | `{kind, scope, statement, rationale?, range?, on_conflict?}` — save ONE idea in the user's words; later runs carry it wherever it applies. Needs `knowledge:write` (+`data:read`) and the member role. NOT human-only: it changes no stored value and no table, only what future runs are told. The server mints `id` and stamps `origin`; never send them. `409 knowledge_conflict` (`error.details.conflicts`) when it contradicts or repeats an active entry: **ask your human**, then resend with `on_conflict: "supersede"` or `"keep_both"`. Send an `Idempotency-Key` |
| `PATCH /v1/blueprints/{blueprint_id}/knowledge/{kid}` | change fields you send (`range: null` clears); send the `revision` you edited to get `409 knowledge_revision_conflict` instead of overwriting a newer edit |
| `POST /v1/blueprints/{blueprint_id}/knowledge/{kid}/retire` | `{reason?}` — stop using an entry in future runs; it is kept, and can be restored |
| `POST /v1/blueprints/{blueprint_id}/knowledge/{kid}/restore` | `{on_conflict?}` — use a retired or replaced entry again; the conflict check runs as for a new one |
| `DELETE /v1/blueprints/{blueprint_id}/knowledge/{kid}` | delete an entry; what runs already found does not change, and it stays on earlier Blueprint versions. A run keeps the wording it was created with |
| `GET /v1/research/runs/{run_id}/knowledge` | the knowledge a run carried, in the wording it had when the run was created, and per entry: `carried` and `dropped` (calls whose prompt held it, or could not fit it), `cited` (QA verdicts that said it changed their verdict: the model's claim, kept only when that cell's prompt carried it) and `decided` with `decided_by` (outcomes code took because of it). Per run: `cells_cited` and `cells_decided` (distinct cells, a cell being one record's column: its value and any also-stated or held row count once, as does a record merged into another before the run's last update; counted once even across a resume, and as of that last update; `cells_cited` keeps a cell a later verdict no longer cites), `decided_by_outcome`, `refused_refs`. **Never report `carried` or `cited` as "decided"**. Outcomes: `held_value` (a changed value held unchecked), `range_flag` (a value outside an entry's range, flagged), `source_refused` (a value resting only on caution hosts, not written: a refused correction or update, or one of a run's values that ranked lower because of the entry; the pick's are under `by_stage.persist`), `source_downscored` (a supported verdict on caution hosts only, capped). `recorded: false` = what the run carried was not recorded (a run from before runs recorded their knowledge, one whose knowledge could not be recorded when it was created, or one whose record could not be read or kept while it ran), not "carried none". `snapshot_entries`: how many entries the run's snapshot holds (`null` = none): with `recorded: false`, above 0 means the run HAD knowledge whose record was lost. Needs `runs:read` |

⚠️ **Knowledge is guidance, never evidence**: no run cites it or fills a value from it. Write
only what your human STATED or you verified from a source; never invent expertise. Any write
can answer `503 blueprint_busy` (another write to the same Blueprint is in progress):
retriable, honour `Retry-After`.

**Applying a change to an active Blueprint is human-only.** You may *ask* what a
change would be (`POST /v1/blueprints/{blueprint_id}/propose`); only a person may make it, and
the gate refuses every key regardless of scope. A missing column is usually
`green` — free and auto-applied — so check before telling a user something
cannot be done.

Checking whether a column exists, and handing the change to a person, are both
covered once in **"Handing a schema change to your human"** below — including
why `GET /v1/blueprints/{blueprint_id}` answers that and
`GET /v1/reports/columns` does not. Read it
there rather than here; a second copy of a rule is the one that goes stale.

## Content reviews (a request the content screen held)
A request DeepSieve's content screen stops answers `422 content_refused`. `error.details.outcome` is `refused` (not reviewable: tell your human, and do not resend it reworded to get around it) or `held` (a person can approve it, and then the SAME request succeeds). A held request's `error.details.review` is `{id, status, submit}`: `submit` is the call that asks for a review while `status` is `not_submitted`, and null after. Nothing is charged for a held or refused request. A held scheduled update or re-check fails, uncharged, and is submitted for review automatically; its run id is in the review's `run_ids`, and once approved its next scheduled update goes ahead.

| Endpoint | Notes |
|---|---|
| `GET /v1/content-reviews` | the organization's reviews, newest first. `status`: `not_submitted`, `submitted`, `approved`, `denied`, `refused`, `unavailable`. Each has `fields` (opaque ids to show your human, never parse them), `run_ids`, `message` (the decision's words; null until the copy is signed off) and `actions[]` (the submit call while `not_submitted`). Never the held text, the rule or a category. `?status=&limit=&cursor=`. `runs:read` |
| `GET /v1/content-reviews/{review_id}` | one review; another organization's is `404`. `runs:read` |
| `POST /v1/content-reviews/{review_id}/submit` | **ask your human first**: a DeepSieve reviewer reads the request's text. `{note?}` (≤1000, staff only). Target one business day. Idempotent while `submitted`; `409 review_closed` once decided, refused or unavailable. `runs:write` + member |

## Handing a schema change to your human

`intake.ignored_refs` means that name is not a column of the Blueprint. A
near-miss the server can pin to one real column is corrected and comes back
under `intake.corrections` instead; one it cannot pin down (the correction
exists on several entities and your `@entity` token resolved to none of them)
is reported here under your own spelling, so check the spelling and the entity
before asking for a column. **Adding it is free**: a new nullable column applies automatically and
costs nothing; only *collecting* the data takes a run.

**You cannot add it yourself. Applying a change to a live Blueprint is
human-only, by design** — a schema edit can drop columns and destroy researched
data, so it goes through a data-safety review your human sees and confirms.

**You CAN ask what the change would be**, before spending their attention:
`POST /v1/blueprints/{blueprint_id}/propose` with `{message}` returns the proposed
Blueprint, the `tier` it would classify as, and why. It writes nothing. Use it to confirm
the change is real and free rather than guessing — and note `tier: null`
alongside a proposal means it could not be classified, which is **not** `green`,
so do not tell your human it is free.

Then hand them a request they only have to read and send:

```
{app_url}/blueprint?ask=<url-encoded request>
```

`app_url` comes from `GET /v1/me`. That opens their Blueprint editor with your
request **pre-filled and not sent** — they read it, edit it, send it, and the
safety gate still decides.

⚠️ **Say what it is, and do not overstate it.** The link carries a *request in
words*, not your proposal. Their chat re-derives the change from that text, so
the result is theirs, not a copy of yours.

⚠️ **Give them a single self-contained request that stands on its own — not
your last message from a longer conversation.** Their chat re-derives from that
text alone, so anything that only makes sense given your earlier turns is lost.
If you cannot reduce your proposal to one such request, **say so plainly**
rather than sending your final turn and assuming it carries.

⚠️ **Never offer an alternative you have not verified.** If you tell your human
"or use `X` instead", check `X` is really a column first —
**`GET /v1/blueprints/{blueprint_id}` is the source that answers that**: it lists each entity with its real columns.

⚠️ **Do not verify against `GET /v1/reports/columns`.** It answers a different
question — what this *report* can show — and it is wrong in both directions for
this purpose: it OMITS the always-shown columns (`name`, `primary_source_urls`,
which are real and are exactly the ones a run reports as unresearched), and it
ADDS relationship pills (`type: "relation"`) that are display constructs, not
columns. Offer one of those as an alternative and it comes back in
`ignored_refs`.

An unverified alternative reads as a working escape hatch and fails *after*
they have committed to it, which is worse than the gap you were reporting. **If
there is no genuine alternative, say nothing** — a limit stated plainly is more
useful than a suggestion that does not hold.

⚠️ **Keep the request SHORT — under ~400 characters.** Two caps bite, and both
fail silently. The editor truncates a pre-filled request at 4000 characters
mid-word; and if your human is signed OUT, the login round-trip carries the
destination in a parameter capped at 512 characters, so a long request is
dropped and they land on an empty editor. One clear sentence survives both; a
pasted specification does not.

⚠️ **They may not be permitted to make the change.** Editing a live Blueprint
requires an **admin** role, and an organization on a managed plan may have
self-serve editing disabled entirely. A viewer or member can open your link,
read it, send it, and be refused at the point of applying. If you know their
role, say who needs to do this; if you do not, do not promise the change will
land.

⚠️ **The link is not guaranteed to resolve.** `app_url` falls back to
`http://localhost:3002` on a deployment that never configured its public URL —
well-formed, and reachable only by the operator. Nothing can detect that for
you. Offer the link; do not promise it works.
